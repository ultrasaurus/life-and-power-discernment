Religious pacifism as a positive way of life rather than as a negative attitude toward fighting, can be considered to be a direct derivative from worship. True worship which pierces through the surface of the mind where multiplicity lies, finds in the depths, beyond words and even thoughts, what George Fox called “the hidden unity in the Eternal Being.” Here the worshipper feels as a present experience rather than as abstract theory his kinship with his fellow men in God. The early Friends seldom used the phrase “joined to the Lord” without adding its complementary expression “and to one another.” Out of this felt unity there comes a sensitizing of the soul, a feeling of oneness with all men which rules out conflict. A new and positive word for pacifism is community. 

-- Howard Brinton, [Sources of the Quaker Peace Testimony](https://inwardlight.org/witness/peace/sources-of-the-quaker-peace-testimony/)
